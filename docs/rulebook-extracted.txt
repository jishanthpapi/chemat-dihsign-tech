WHAT HAPPENS
1. Regular turns
Roll for pesos; buy, trade, bank and queue Action Cards.
2. Action Resolution
Defence first, then Attack, then Good/Chaos. Keep play order within each group.
3. Shared Reaction Phase
At most one compound per active player, in normal order.
4. Double Reaction mini-rounds
Each eligible player takes all awarded mini-rounds consecutively.

• CHEMa’t™ uses:
 
Reactant Cards
Apparatus Cards
Action Cards
Task Cards (showing compounds to create)
A standard six-sided die
Peso coins or tokens
One personal piggy bank per player
One shared central bank
A way to track each player's score

Card deck sizes

 individual Action Card quantities add up to 64, not 62. This list preserves those individual quantities.

02 Objective and Winning
• The goal is to reach 10 points by successfully completing compounds shown on your Task Cards.
 
• Compound difficulty determines points:
 
• Easy: 1 point
 
• Medium: 3 points
 
• Hard: 5 points
 
• The first player to reach at least 10 points secures first place.
 
• Remaining players continue competing for second, third, and fourth place.
 
• Players continue until only one player remains without a secured finishing position.
 
• If several players reach 10 points during the same Reaction Phase, the player who reaches it first in the normal Reaction
 Phase turn order receives the higher placement.
• A player who reaches 10 points normally stops playing immediately.
 
• That player discards all remaining cards into the appropriate discard piles and returns all their pesos, including
 piggy-bank savings, to the central bank.
• Any unused Double Reaction opportunities are forfeited when the player leaves.
 
Winning with outstanding loans
• A player who reaches 10 points while owing a loan cannot leave the game yet.
 
• Their finishing position is secured immediately.
 
• Their score freezes at 10 points.
 
• They must continue playing until all outstanding loans and overdue penalties are fully repaid.
 
• They may continue rolling for pesos, buying cards, trading, banking, and playing Action Cards.
 
• They may also create compounds voluntarily, but these compounds earn zero additional points.
 
• These compounds still require all necessary cards and the normal creation fee.
 
• Once every debt is repaid, the player officially exits and returns remaining resources according to the normal exit rules.

03 Game Setup
• Place the shared central bank in the middle of the playing area.
 
• Give every player an empty personal piggy bank.
 
• Separate the Reactant, Apparatus, Action, and Task Cards into their respective decks.
 
• Shuffle each deck separately.
 
• Place each deck face down.
 
• Make space for a separate face-up discard pile beside each deck.
 
• Randomly choose the first player.
 
• Determine the initial player turn order.
 
• Every player starts with 0 pesos.
 
• Each player rolls the six-sided die once and receives that number of pesos from the central bank.
 
• Shuffle the Task deck.
 
• All players close their eyes and take one random Task Card each.
 
• Players privately inspect their Task Cards.
 
• Remaining Task Cards form the face-down Task draw pile.
 
• Each player starts with one active Task Card.
 
• The game begins with the first player's regular turn.

04 Normal Rounds
• Each shared round consists of three stages.
 
Stage 1 - Regular Turns
• Every active player gets one regular turn in the established player order.
 
• At the beginning of their turn, the player rolls the die.
 
• They receive the rolled number of pesos from the central bank.
 
• They may buy cards, trade, bank money, and play Action Cards.
 
• They may perform as many affordable permitted actions as they want.
 
• They may end their turn whenever they wish.
 
• Players may also end their turns without buying or playing anything.
 
• Action Cards played during regular turns have their effects queued until the Action Resolution stage.
 
• Creating compounds is not allowed during regular turns.
 
Stage 2 - Action Resolution
• After all players finish their regular turns, resolve pending Action Card effects.
 
• Resolve Defence Cards first, preserving their original play order.
 
• Resolve Attack Cards next, preserving their original play order.
 
• Resolve Good and Chaos Cards afterward, preserving their original play order within this combined group.
 
• Resolve applicable redirections, protections, and other interactions according to the Defence rules.
 
• If an Action Card cannot successfully produce its effect, that effect fails.
 
• No Action Card playing fee is ever refunded, even if the effect fails.
 
Stage 3 - Reaction Phase
• After all pending Action Card effects have resolved, the shared Reaction Phase begins.
 
• Players receive their Reaction Phase opportunities in normal player turn order.
 
• Each player may successfully create a maximum of one compound per Reaction Phase.
 
• Players may only create the compound shown on their own active Task Card.
 
• Players must have every required Reactant and Apparatus Card and enough pesos for the creation fee.
 
• A player may instead keep their task for a later round or voluntarily replace it according to the Task replacement rules.
 
• The Reaction Phase ends after every active player has finished their opportunity.
 
• After the shared Reaction Phase ends, resolve any Double Reaction mini-rounds awarded for that round.
 
• Once those mini-rounds are finished, the next shared round begins.

05 Pesos and the Central Bank
• Pesos are the game's currency.
 
• Each player starts with the number of pesos rolled during setup.
 
• Players roll the six-sided die at the beginning of every regular turn and gain that number of pesos.
 
• The same income roll applies to extra regular turns awarded by Double Reaction.
 
• Pesos used to buy cards are paid to the shared central bank.
 
• Pesos used to play Action Cards are paid to the central bank.
 
• Pesos used for compound-creation fees are paid to the central bank.
 
• There is no general limit on how many pesos a player may hold.
 
• Pesos can be traded, gifted, loaned, or deposited into personal piggy banks.
 
06 Buying Cards
• Cards may be purchased during regular turns only.
 
• Players may buy as many cards as they can afford.
 
• The cost of one Reactant Card is 1 peso.
 
• The cost of one Apparatus Card is 1 peso.
 
• The cost of one Action Card is 2 pesos.
 
• Players buy the top face-down card of the appropriate deck.
 
• Players cannot look through a draw deck to select a specific card.
 
• Purchased cards go into the player's hand.
 
• Newly purchased Action Cards can be played during the same regular turn.
 
• Buying an Action Card and playing it are separate payments.
 
• Task Cards cannot be purchased.
 
• Cards cannot be purchased during Reaction Phases.

07 Personal Piggy Banks
• Every player has one personal piggy bank.
 
• A player may deposit any amount of pesos.
 
• There is no maximum amount of piggy-bank savings.
 
• Every deposit costs a flat 1-peso fee, regardless of the amount deposited.
 
• The fee must be paid from pesos available outside the piggy bank.
 
• If the player cannot pay the fee, they cannot make the deposit.
 
• Withdrawals are free.
 
• A player may withdraw as much of their own saved money as they want.
 
• Banking is permitted during regular turns, including other players' turns.
 
• Banking is permitted before any player begins creating a compound during the shared Reaction Phase.
 
• Once the first player begins compound creation, banking is closed for the remainder of that shared Reaction Phase.
 
• Banking is allowed during Double Reaction mini-rounds, subject to the same restrictions.
 
• Money in the piggy bank may be withdrawn to pay for cards, actions, trades, or loans when banking is permitted.
 
• When a player officially exits the game, all remaining piggy-bank money returns to the central bank.

08 Trading and Gifts
• Players may trade Reactant Cards, Apparatus Cards, Action Cards, and pesos.
 
• Any combination of eligible cards and pesos may be exchanged.
 
• Players may negotiate the terms of their trades.
 
• A trade must be mutually agreed upon.
 
• Once both parties agree and complete a trade, it is binding.
 
• Task Cards cannot be traded.
 
• Players may trade away cards for pesos.
 
• Players may gift cards or pesos without receiving anything in return.
 
• Trades may happen during a player's regular turn or during another player's regular turn.
 
• During a shared Reaction Phase, trading is allowed only before the first player begins creating compounds.
 
• Once compound creation begins, no more trading is permitted during that shared Reaction Phase.
 
• During Double Reaction mini-rounds, only the player taking the extra regular turn is allowed to trade; another player may
 participate as the other party to that trade.
• Trades must follow all banking restrictions.
 
• If a card needed for a Task is traded away or stolen, the affected player must obtain another copy before completing the
 Task.
• There is no hand-size limit.
 
• Players may possess multiple copies of the same card.
 
09 Loans
• Players may lend pesos or eligible cards to other players.
 
• Both parties must agree to the loan.
 
• They must agree on the amount or resources borrowed.
 
• They must agree on a fixed number of shared rounds within which repayment must occur.
 
• The borrower is responsible for repaying the loan under the agreed terms.
 
• Only completed shared rounds count toward repayment deadlines.
 
• Double Reaction mini-rounds do not count as additional rounds for repayment deadlines.
 
• If repayment is not completed by the deadline, the borrower owes an additional 2 pesos per overdue shared round.
 
• Overdue penalties continue accumulating while the loan remains overdue.
 
• Players may repay loans during permitted trading or banking windows.
 
• Loan obligations are not cancelled simply because a player reaches 10 points.
 
• A player with unpaid debts must remain in the game after reaching 10 points.
 
• Their score remains frozen at 10.
 
• They may continue normal economic gameplay to repay their debt.
 
• They cannot officially exit until all outstanding loans and penalties are settled.

10 Task Cards
• Task Cards represent the compounds players must create.
 
• Each player has exactly one active Task Card at a time.
 
• Task Cards are normally kept secret.
 
• Players may voluntarily reveal their own Task Cards.
 
• Players may also lie about which compound they are trying to create.
 
• Other players cannot force a player to reveal their Task Card.
 
• Task Cards cannot be traded or stolen.
 
• A player may only create the compound shown on their active Task Card.
 
• Players cannot create a different compound for points.
 
• Each Task Card may be completed only once.
 
• When a Task is successfully completed, the player earns the appropriate points.
 
• The completed Task Card goes into the Task discard pile.
 
• The player draws a new Task Card at the end of their Reaction Phase turn.
 
• Because players are limited to one successful compound per Reaction Phase, a new Task cannot be completed until a
 later Reaction Phase.
• If a player cannot complete their Task, they retain it for a future round.
 
Voluntary Task replacement
• Players may voluntarily replace their Task Card during their Reaction Phase opportunity.
 
• Voluntary replacement is not allowed during regular turns.
 
• The first voluntary replacement costs 20 pesos.
 
• Each later voluntary replacement costs 5 pesos more than the previous one.
 
• The costs are therefore 20, 25, 30, 35 pesos, and so on.
 
• The replacement count does not reset after successfully completing a Task Card.
 
• The old Task Card is placed in the Task discard pile.
 
• The player immediately draws a new Task Card.
 
• The replacement Task Card cannot be completed during that same Reaction Phase.
 
• The player must wait until the next Reaction Phase.
 
• Replacing a Task Card does not discard any held Reactant or Apparatus Cards.
 
• The same replacement rules and fees apply during personal Double Reaction Reaction Phases.

11 Creating Compounds
• Compounds may be created only during Reaction Phases.
 
• The player must have the active Task Card for the compound.
 
• The player must have every Reactant Card listed on that Task.
 
• The player must have every Apparatus Card listed on that Task.
 
• Each listed reactant name requires one card, regardless of the coefficients in a real balanced chemical equation.
 
• The player must pay the compound-creation fee.
 
• The creation fee is 5 pesos plus 1 peso for each required Reactant Card and 1 peso for each required Apparatus Card.
 
• The fee is paid to the central bank.
 
• The required Reactant Cards are consumed and placed in the Reactant discard pile.
 
• The required Apparatus Cards are also consumed and placed in the Apparatus discard pile.
 
• Apparatus cannot be reused after creating a compound unless the player obtains another copy.
 
• The successfully completed Task Card goes into the Task discard pile.
 
• The player receives the points corresponding to its difficulty, subject to Double Yield or the frozen-score rule.
 
• The player draws a replacement Task Card at the end of their Reaction Phase turn.
 
• A player may successfully create only one compound in each Reaction Phase.
 
• A personal Double Reaction Reaction Phase gives that player a separate opportunity to create one compound.
 
• If the player lacks the required cards or money, they cannot create the compound.
 
• Real laboratory quantities and procedures are not needed: recipes are game-card requirements.
 
Example
• To make Calcium carbonate:
 
• Calcium chloride: 1 Reactant Card
 
• Sodium carbonate: 1 Reactant Card
 
• Beaker: 1 Apparatus Card
 
• Stirring rod: 1 Apparatus Card
 
• Creation fee: 5 + 2 reactants + 2 apparatus = 9 pesos
 
• Reward: 1 point, unless a multiplier applies

12 Action Cards - General Rules
• All Action Cards cost 2 pesos to buy.
 
• Playing an Action Card costs an additional 2 pesos.
 
• Buying and playing one Action Card in the same turn therefore costs 4 pesos total.
 
• Action Cards may be played only during regular turns.
 
• Action Cards cannot be played during Reaction Phases.
 
• Players may play multiple Action Cards in one regular turn.
 
• Players may play multiple copies of the same Action Card.
 
• Action Cards can be played during the regular turn in which they were purchased.
 
• Action Card effects normally remain pending until the Action Resolution stage.
 
• Defence effects resolve before Attack effects.
 
• Good and Chaos effects resolve after attacks.
 
• Original play order is preserved within the established resolution groups.
 
• An Action Card that cannot produce its effect simply fails.
 
• Playing fees are never refunded.
 
• Once an Action Card has resolved or expired, it goes into the Action discard pile, unless its effect requires a different
 handling.
• Action Cards cannot affect a player's Task Card unless a specifically agreed rule explicitly permits it. No current Action
 Card permits this.

General Defence rules
Defence Cards resolve before Attack Cards, regardless of the order they were played.
 
Multiple Defence Cards may be active simultaneously.
 
Defences operate according to their specific conditions.
 
When several attacks are pending, Safety Goggles cancels the first eligible attack in resolution order.
 
A card blocked by Secure Storage is not considered stolen.
 
If Secure Storage completely prevents a theft, Emergency Backup does not activate for that theft.
 
Safety Goggles is not consumed by a theft already completely prevented by Secure Storage.
 
Emergency Backup's owner chooses which affected card to save.
 
Lab Insurance checks the number of cards actually lost after all protective effects.
 
Losing only 1 card does not activate Lab Insurance.
 
If Emergency Backup reduces a 2-card loss to 1 card, Lab Insurance does not activate.
 
Paying for an unused Defence Card does not earn a refund.
 
No Defence Card can protect against a Chaos Card.

16 Attack and Defence Interactions
• Safety Goggles vs. Card Heist: Safety Goggles cancels Card Heist against its owner if it is the first eligible attack.
 
• Secure Storage vs. Card Heist: Secure Storage blocks the theft; the attacker receives no refund.
 
• Safety Goggles + Secure Storage: Secure Storage handles card theft first. If it completely blocks the theft, Safety
 Goggles stays unused and available against a later eligible attack.
• Emergency Backup vs. Card Heist: If 2 cards are selected to be stolen, the victim chooses 1 to keep; the remaining card
 is stolen.
• Lab Insurance + Emergency Backup: Lab Insurance checks actual losses after Emergency Backup. If only 1 card is lost,
 Lab Insurance does not trigger.
• Counter-Reaction vs. theft: The attack can be redirected to another player.
 
• Counter-Reaction vs. Secure Storage: If the final target has Secure Storage and the redirected effect is card theft, Secure
 Storage blocks it.
• Counter-Reaction vs. Safety Goggles: Unused Safety Goggles on the final target can cancel the redirected attack if it is
 the next eligible attack.
• Supply Shortage vs. Secure Storage: Secure Storage does not prevent forced discards.
 
• Supply Shortage vs. Safety Goggles: Safety Goggles protects its owner from their portion of Supply Shortage. The other
 affected players still discard normally.
• Supply Shortage vs. Emergency Backup: Emergency Backup may save 1 Reactant Card that would otherwise be
 discarded.
• Supply Shortage vs. Counter-Reaction: An affected player may redirect their individual discard effect to another player.
 
• If the new target was already affected by Supply Shortage, they can be required to discard 2 Reactant Cards total.
 
• Supply Shortage vs. Lab Insurance: If a player actually loses 2 Reactant Cards after applicable defences, Lab Insurance
 activates and they draw 2 replacement Reactant Cards.
• Multiple Counter-Reactions may redirect the same pending attack in sequence.
 
• Only the final target receives the redirected effect, except for the original independent effects of an area attack such as
 Supply Shortage.
• An attack that is successfully cancelled or otherwise cannot affect its final target produces no effect against that target.
 
• No cancelled or failed Action Card earns its playing fee back.

Chaos-specific rules
Chaos Cards cannot be defended against.
 
Safety Goggles cannot cancel Chaos effects.

Chaos effects still follow general card-availability restrictions.

The person playing Market Swap chooses which of their cards they will give away.

A player with fewer than 2 available cards may still participate.

If either participant has no eligible cards, no exchange can occur.

18 Double Reaction - Complete Rules
• Double Reaction is a Chaos Action Card.
 
• Playing it costs 2 pesos, in addition to its 2-peso purchase cost.
 
• Its effect is resolved under normal Action Card timing.
 
• It grants the player one extra mini-round after the current shared Reaction Phase finishes.
 
• A mini-round consists of:
 
• One extra regular turn.
 
• One personal Reaction Phase.
 
• During the extra regular turn, the player rolls the die and collects pesos.
 
• They may buy cards, bank pesos, trade, and play Action Cards.
 
• Only the extra-turn player may initiate trading during their mini-round.
 
• During the personal Reaction Phase, only that player can create a compound.
 
• The player may create a maximum of one compound in that personal Reaction Phase.
 
• Their compound must match their current Task Card.
 
• All normal creation costs apply.
 
• Banking and Task replacement are permitted under the normal timing rules.
 
• A player may hold and play multiple Double Reaction cards.
 
• Every successfully resolved Double Reaction grants a separate mini-round.
 
• When multiple players earn mini-rounds during the same shared round, resolve them in normal player order.
 
• Each eligible player completes all their awarded mini-rounds consecutively before the next eligible player starts theirs.
 
• Mini-rounds do not count as full shared rounds for loan repayment deadlines.
 
• Action Cards played during mini-rounds are not resolved immediately.
 
• Those Action Cards remain pending until the Action Resolution stage of the next full shared round.
 
• Consequently, an Action Card played during an extra regular turn does not affect that mini-round's immediately following
 personal Reaction Phase.
• If Double Reaction itself is played during a mini-round, its effect resolves at the end of the next shared regular-turn stage.
 
• The newly awarded mini-round then occurs after the next shared Reaction Phase, not immediately.
 
• After all scheduled mini-rounds finish, normal shared-round play resumes.
 
• A player who officially exits after reaching 10 points forfeits unused mini-rounds.
 
Example
• If player order is A > B > C > D, Player B earns 3 Double Reactions, and Player D earns 2:
 
• Complete the current shared Reaction Phase.
 
• Player B takes mini-round 1: regular turn and personal Reaction Phase.
 
• Player B takes mini-round 2.
 
• Player B takes mini-round 3.
 
• Player D takes mini-round 1.
 
• Player D takes mini-round 2.
 
• Begin the next shared round.
 
• Any Action Cards played during these mini-rounds remain pending until the next shared round's Action Resolution stage.

19 Decks, Discard Piles and Card Availability
• Every card type has its own draw deck and discard pile.
 
• Draw decks are face down.
 
• Discard piles are face up.
 
• Players may inspect discard piles at any time.
 
• Used Reactant Cards go to the Reactant discard pile.
 
• Used Apparatus Cards go to the Apparatus discard pile.
 
• Used or expired Action Cards go to the Action discard pile.
 
• Completed and voluntarily replaced Task Cards go to the Task discard pile.
 
• When a draw deck runs out, shuffle its corresponding discard pile into a new draw deck.
 
• This applies to the Reactant, Apparatus, Action, and Task decks.
 
• If both a draw deck and its discard pile are empty, cards cannot be drawn from that type until cards become available.
 
• If a card effect requires drawing more cards than are available, draw as many as possible.
 
• There is no refund for an Action Card that cannot draw its full intended amount.
 
• Discard piles can be accessed directly only when a card or rule permits it.
 
• Taking from a discard pile does not require shuffling it unless the associated deck must be replenished.

24 Consolidated Important Rules
• You can only create compounds during Reaction Phases.
 
• You can only create the compound shown on your active Task Card.
 
• You can successfully complete a maximum of one compound per Reaction Phase.
 
• Every Reactant and Apparatus Card used to complete a compound is consumed.
 
• Every successful compound requires a creation fee.
 
• Task Cards are secret unless their owners choose to reveal them.
 
• Players may lie about their Tasks.
 
• Task Cards cannot be bought, traded, or stolen.
 
• Action Cards cost 2 pesos to buy and an additional 2 pesos to play.
 
• Action Cards can be played only during regular turns.
 
• Action Card effects normally resolve at the end of all regular turns.
 
• Defence resolves before Attack, and Good/Chaos resolves afterward.
 
• Failed Action Cards never receive refunds.
 
• Chaos Cards cannot be defended against.
 
• Players may hold unlimited cards and duplicate cards.
 
• Discard piles are public.
 
• Trading is permitted before compound creation starts, but not afterward during that Reaction Phase.
 
• Banking follows the same Reaction Phase cutoff.
 
• Voluntary Task replacement has an increasing cost and forces the new task to wait until a later Reaction Phase.
 
• Double Yield applies only during its eligible current shared Reaction Phase and can stack.
 
• Double Reaction grants an extra regular turn followed by a private Reaction Phase.
 
• Multiple Double Reaction mini-rounds are completed consecutively by each eligible player in normal order.
 
• Action Cards played during mini-rounds resolve in the next shared round.
 
• Players may negotiate loans with fixed repayment deadlines.
 
• Overdue loans incur a 2-peso penalty per overdue shared round.
 
• Reaching 10 points with unpaid loans secures placement but does not end participation.
 
• Players with frozen scores may still create compounds, but those compounds earn zero points.
 
• Players exit only after reaching 10 points and settling outstanding loans.
 
• After an eligible player exits, the remaining players continue competing for the remaining positions.
 
• Decks are replenished by reshuffling their corresponding discard piles when necessary.

25 Details Not Yet Explicitly Defined
• The following points have not been given fully specific rules. They should be treated as open design details rather than
 silently invented by a rulebook editor.
• Loans involving cards: What exact form of repayment is required if an equivalent card is unavailable? The overdue
 penalty is specified in pesos.
• How loan deadlines are recorded: Players need a consistent way to record the lender, borrower, amount owed, and due
 shared round.
• Several simultaneous loan debts: A precise priority for repayment has not been established.
 
• Multiple Experiment Reversal cards: The exact handling of multiple reversals resolving during the same stage should be
 specified. Applying each reversal in sequence is the straightforward interpretation.
• Formula Shuffle and empty hands: A player without any eligible card cannot pass a card, but the treatment of the
 remaining players' passes has not been explicitly decided.
• Card inventories: The agreed quantities total 64 Action Cards, despite an earlier total of 62. The individual quantities
 listed above are the more complete specification.
• Chemistry accuracy: The compounds and simplified recipes are the agreed game-card recipes, not verified laboratory
 synthesis instructions. Scientific accuracy should be audited separately before the cards are printed.
Additional unresolved edge cases
• Loan bookkeeping: the exact checkpoint for a shared round to count as completed, when the first overdue penalty is
 charged, and the recipient of penalties are not specified.
• Lender exit: handling repayment to a lender who has already left is not specified. Secured placements do not override the
 latest requirement that borrowers keep playing until all debts are paid; the procedure after only one unplaced player
remains is not specified.
• Counter-Reaction: consumption, expiry, automatic versus chosen activation, and priority against other applicable
 defences are not fully specified. Redirection chains are allowed, but a precise termination rule is absent.
• Repeated defences: stacking and consumption rules for multiple copies of the same Defence Card are not fully specified.
 
• Equipment Theft: who selects the stolen Apparatus Card is not specified.
 
• Empty Task supply: the rules allow partial draws, but do not explain how exactly one active Task is restored if both Task
 piles are empty.
• Research Breakthrough: partial draws are permitted, but the keep/discard split with fewer than five available cards is not
 specified.
• Replacement frequency and end-of-phase windows: the number of voluntary replacements per opportunity, and a
 trading/banking cutoff if nobody creates a compound, are not specified.
Theme note
Taco, nacho, churro and related designs are illustrations or card-holder motifs. No real food is needed to play.
